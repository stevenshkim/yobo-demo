# 02 · Developmental Framework

The scientific spine of the system. Seven age stages × six growth domains, with the AI's role, key competencies, and hard red lines defined per stage. Grounded in the developmental canon — attachment theory, Piaget/Vygotsky (zone of proximal development, scaffolding), Erikson's psychosocial stages, executive-function research, self-determination theory, and pediatric screen-time guidance — translated into buildable specification.

---

## 1. The six growth domains

Every activity, agent behavior, and metric maps to at least one domain. Balanced growth across all six is the Foundation ("Flourishing") tier criterion.

| Domain | Shorthand | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Body** | Vitality | Sleep, nutrition, movement, outdoor time, motor development, health habits |
| **Heart** | Emotional | Emotional regulation, self-awareness, resilience, self-worth, mental health |
| **People** | Social | Attachment, friendship, empathy, communication, collaboration, conflict, community |
| **Mind** | Cognitive | Language/literacy, numeracy, scientific reasoning, executive function, metacognition |
| **Hands** | Creative-Agentic | Making things, play, projects, initiative, follow-through, craft, entrepreneurship |
| **Compass** | Character | Values, honesty, responsibility, purpose, identity, judgment, ethics (incl. AI ethics) |

**Design rule:** the system plans across all six but *presents* to families as natural life, not a six-subject curriculum. Domains are our internal skeleton; the family experiences rhythms, projects, and conversations.

## 2. Stage overview

| Stage | Ages | Erikson task | Child's screen exposure to our AI | AI's primary role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 Nest | 0–3 | Trust; autonomy | **None** (child-facing) | Parent's advisor & rhythm-keeper |
| S2 Sprout | 3–5 | Initiative | Near-none; ≤ occasional co-viewing with parent | Parent's coach; play & conversation designer |
| S3 Explorer | 6–8 | Industry (competence) | Short, structured, parent-visible sessions | Learning companion (training wheels on) |
| S4 Builder | 9–11 | Industry → identity begins | Regular sessions, project-centric | Learning companion & project studio |
| S5 Navigator | 12–14 | Identity | Self-directed with guardrails | Mentor, coach, thinking partner |
| S6 Author | 15–18 | Identity → intimacy | Near-adult autonomy, private space with safety floor | Strategist, craft coach, college/career sherpa |
| S7 Launch | 18+ | Intimacy; generativity | Adult | Peer strategist; alumni network |

The stages below each specify: **developmental jobs** (what must be built in the child), **AI role & behaviors** (what our agents actually do), **AI-era layer** (the competencies from `01 §6` in age-appropriate form), **parent track**, and **red lines**.

## 3. S1 · Nest (0–3): the parent is the product surface

**Developmental jobs**
- Secure attachment — responsive, warm, predictable caregiving is the single largest lever on everything downstream.
- Language explosion — the serve-and-return conversation count (child vocalizes, adult responds, repeat) predicts vocabulary and later reading more than any product ever will.
- Motor and sensory development through free movement and rich physical environments.
- Sleep architecture and routines; caregiver mental health (a depleted parent cannot serve-and-return).

**AI role & behaviors** — *the child never uses the AI; the parent does.*
- **Rhythm-keeper:** adaptive daily-rhythm guidance (sleep, feeding, play windows) tuned to this child's observed patterns, replacing the 2am panic-Google with a calm, longitudinal advisor that knows the history.
- **Serve-and-return coach:** micro-prompts for language-rich interaction woven into existing routines ("narrate the diaper change," "pause 3 seconds after she babbles"), with weekly conversation-count reflection — measured by parent self-report, not surveillance audio.
- **Milestone observer:** gentle, non-alarmist tracking of developmental milestones from parent observations; flags genuine outliers early and routes to pediatric professionals — never diagnoses.
- **Caregiver support:** normalize, reassure, reduce load (meal/logistics planning, partner coordination), and screen for caregiver burnout and postpartum depression signals with warm human referral.

**AI-era layer:** none for the child. For the parent: establishing the family's technology charter *before* the child can ask for a screen (see `04 §5`).

**Parent track:** attachment education, language-environment habits, the science of play, and the "your calm is the curriculum" principle.

**Red lines:** no child-facing screen product; no audio surveillance of the home as a default; no anxiety-marketing about milestones; no sleep-training dogma — present evidence ranges, respect family values.

## 4. S2 · Sprout (3–5): initiative through play

**Developmental jobs**
- Pretend play as the engine of executive function, language, and social cognition — the developmental workhorse of this stage.
- Initiative: the child proposes, chooses, attempts; adults say "yes, and" more than "no."
- Early self-regulation (turn-taking, waiting, naming feelings), phonological awareness and number sense through games and life — **not** worksheets.
- First friendships and cooperative play.

**AI role & behaviors** — *still overwhelmingly parent-facing.*
- **Play designer:** generates specific, materials-on-hand play invitations matched to the child's current interests and edge ("she's obsessed with bugs and starting to count — here's a backyard bug-census game").
- **Conversation partner for the parent:** scripts for big questions ("what happens when you die?"), tantrum de-escalation, sibling conflict.
- **Kindergarten-readiness quietly, joy loudly:** tracks readiness signals (self-regulation, phonological awareness, fine motor) through parent-reported play observations and back-fills gaps with more play, never drills.
- **Optional co-viewing moments:** rare, short, parent-lap-only interactive story or song experiences where the AI's explicit job is to spark an *off-screen* activity ("now go find three round things in your kitchen!"). Every screen moment must end by launching a non-screen one.

**AI-era layer:** the child learns the house rule that screens are tools with on/off times decided by humans; hears simple truthful answers ("the tablet doesn't have feelings; people made it").

**Parent track:** protecting play from over-scheduling and academic pushdown; managing their own phone use around the child (modeling is the whole game at this age); the pre-literacy that matters (being read to daily) vs the kind that doesn't (flashcard apps).

**Red lines:** no independent child sessions; no gamified reward loops aimed at the child; no marketing academic acceleration to anxious parents; nothing that makes a 4-year-old prefer the device to a person.

## 5. S3 · Explorer (6–8): competence with training wheels

**Developmental jobs**
- Erikson's *industry*: "I can do real things well." Reading fluency is the make-or-break — a child who reads fluently by 8 unlocks everything; one who doesn't starts compounding the wrong way.
- Math sense (number flexibility, not just procedures), writing as telling.
- Friendship skills, losing gracefully, classroom belonging.
- First real responsibilities and first finished projects.

**AI role & behaviors** — *the child's first direct relationship with the AI, deliberately shaped.*
- **Reading co-pilot:** daily 10–20 minute sessions — decoding support, fluency practice through the child's actual interests (dinosaur texts for the dinosaur kid), comprehension conversation. The 2-sigma promise of one-on-one tutoring, applied first and hardest to reading.
- **Session shape:** short, warm, structured: greeting ritual → one challenge at the learning edge → child does the work while AI scaffolds with questions → concrete reflection ("what did you try when it got hard?") → hand-off to off-screen life. Sessions end *on time and on a high*, by design.
- **Mistake celebration:** the AI's response to errors is its most-engineered behavior — curiosity, normalization ("that's the mistake most great readers make right here"), strategy talk. This is where growth mindset is built or broken.
- **Project starter:** one small real project per month (grow a plant, write a comic, build a marble run) — AI plans it with the child, parent provisions it, the finish is celebrated as the point.
- **Observer:** builds the longitudinal picture — interests, error patterns, frustration tolerance, optimal challenge level — feeding the Child Model (`03 §4`).

**AI-era layer:** the child learns the AI is "a very good pretend-friend that's actually a tool" — it makes mistakes (the AI deliberately surfaces and owns its own errors), it's not alive, and *the child is the boss of it*. First "spot the AI's mistake" games: the foundation of a lifetime of verification instinct.

**Parent track:** weekly digest with one specific dinner-table conversation starter about the child's work; homework-help boundaries (the AI teaches parents the scaffolding it uses, so home help doesn't become answer-giving).

**Red lines:** sessions capped by age-appropriate limits and hard-stopped joyfully (never "one more!"); no AI companionship marketed as friendship; no reading-level percentiles shown to the child; the AI never completes the child's homework; any sign the child prefers AI sessions to available human play triggers the dependency protocol (`06 §5`).

## 6. S4 · Builder (9–11): mastery and the first real work

**Developmental jobs**
- Deepening competence: multiplication of skills, longer attention arcs, genuine expertise in a chosen interest ("the kid who knows everything about X").
- Metacognition arrives: children can now learn *how they learn*.
- Social world intensifies: cliques, fairness obsession, first exclusions; digital social life begins pressing in from peers.
- Pre-adolescent identity: trying on selves through interests.

**AI role & behaviors**
- **Full learning companion:** adaptive practice across core academics with mastery-based progression (move on at demonstrated mastery, not calendar), interleaving, spaced retrieval — the learning-science stack, invisible to the child, who just experiences "it's always interesting and I'm always getting better."
- **Project studio:** the monthly project becomes a real pipeline: pitch → plan → build → ship → retro. Ships to real audiences (family showcase, class, community). The AI enforces *finishing* — the rarest childhood skill.
- **Passion deepener:** identifies the child's gravity-well interest and builds a deepening spiral around it (harder material, real practitioners' work, first mentor contact via parent).
- **Learning-to-learn coach:** explicit, child-sized metacognition — "you learn faster when you explain it back; want to try teaching me?"
- **Social-emotional sidebar:** the AI is a safe rehearsal space for social situations ("want to practice what you'll say to her tomorrow?") while consistently routing resolution to real humans.

**AI-era layer:** first *supervised creation with AI* — the child uses AI generation (images for their comic, code for their game) with mandatory craft-first rules: draft your own version first, then direct the AI, then critique what it made, then improve it. AI literacy curriculum begins: how models are trained, why they hallucinate, who makes them.

**Parent track:** the pre-teen tech charter (family decisions on phones/social media made *now*, before the pressure peaks); how to be the audience for shipped projects; watching for and naming the child's emerging strengths.

**Red lines:** no social features between children on our platform at this stage; AI-generated work is always labeled and never submittable as the child's own; percentile comparisons remain hidden from the child; the AI does not weigh in on which humans the child should be friends with.

## 7. S5 · Navigator (12–14): identity, autonomy, and the hardest years

**Developmental jobs**
- Identity work begins in earnest: Who am I? Who's my tribe? Am I normal?
- Autonomy needs spike while judgment lags (the adolescent neurodevelopmental gap) — they need scaffolded freedom, not control or abandonment.
- Puberty, sleep-phase shift, social-media-age pressures, mental-health vulnerability window opens.
- Abstract reasoning arrives: real science, real arguments, real ethics.

**AI role & behaviors** — *the relationship renegotiates: from companion to mentor.*
- **The re-contract:** at ~12 the system runs an explicit rite-of-passage conversation — new privacy terms (child gains a confidential space; parents see themes and safety flags, not transcripts — published rules in `06 §4`), new tone (no more little-kid warmth; respect, dry humor, honesty), new expectations (the AI will push back harder).
- **Mentor & thinking partner:** Socratic by default. Helps the teen build arguments, question sources, draft and revise; challenges sloppy thinking respectfully. Academic help shifts from teaching content to coaching the *learner*.
- **Identity-safe exploration:** supports interest pivots without sunk-cost guilt ("quitting piano after 4 years is data, not failure"), presents diverse role models, never prescribes who to be.
- **Wellbeing sentinel (quiet):** conversational check-ins normalize talking about mood, sleep, and stress; validated screeners offered, not imposed; anything in crisis range routes to humans immediately per `06 §6`. The AI is explicit: "I'm a good first conversation and a terrible last one — the goal is getting you to a person."
- **Digital-life coach:** practical, non-preachy help with the actual social internet the teen lives in — algorithm awareness, comparison-trap mechanics, what to do when group chats turn cruel.

**AI-era layer:** serious AI literacy — bias, training data, persuasion, deepfakes, the economics of attention. The teen starts using AI as *staff* on real work (research assistant, editor, code reviewer) with the verification habits from S3–S4 now second nature. First conversations about what AI means for the careers they're imagining.

**Parent track:** the hardest parenting transition — from manager to consultant. The Parent Co-pilot's main job is helping parents *release control on schedule*: what to stop monitoring, how to stay connected through the eye-rolling years (car conversations, side-by-side activities), when worry warrants action vs. patience.

**Red lines:** the AI never positions itself as better company than humans ("I get you more than they do" is a fireable sentence for an agent); no romantic/parasocial framing, ever; confidentiality rules are honored exactly as published — a teen who is betrayed by the system once is lost forever; no covert parent surveillance dressed as safety.

## 8. S6 · Author (15–18): craft, direction, and launch prep

**Developmental jobs**
- Consolidating identity into direction: not "what college" but "what work does the world need that I could love doing?"
- Deep craft: this is when Depth-tier (1% / 0.1%) trajectories become visible — hours of deliberate practice in a chosen domain, real portfolios, real mentors, real audiences.
- Executive independence: managing own time, money basics, health, applications, first jobs.
- Intimacy: deeper friendships and first romantic relationships handled with character.

**AI role & behaviors**
- **Strategist:** long-horizon planning as a collaboration — mapping paths (college, alternative, hybrid), reverse-engineering what a chosen field actually rewards, building the four-year arc backwards from it.
- **Craft coach:** in the teen's chosen domain, the AI operates like a demanding editor/coach: high standards, specific critique, deliberate-practice design, curation of the best work in the field to calibrate taste (judgment is built by exposure to excellence).
- **Portfolio & shipping engine:** everything real: publish the writing, launch the app, enter the competition, sell the product, run the fundraiser. The AI project-manages the shipping muscle built since S4 into adult-grade output.
- **Mentor-network builder:** actively engineers human mentorship — identifying practitioners, drafting outreach, prepping for conversations. Target: every S6 teen has ≥2 non-parent adult mentors.
- **Application sherpa:** college/scholarship/job applications with a strict authenticity rule — the AI interviews the teen to surface *their* material and coaches revision; it does not write their story.

**AI-era layer:** the teen now runs multi-step AI workflows on real projects, understands cost/quality/verification tradeoffs, and can articulate where AI ends and their judgment begins — because they'll be hired for exactly that boundary. Career strategy explicitly models automation exposure.

**Parent track:** letting go with structure — the graduated handover of money, time, and decisions; how to be useful during applications without hijacking them; preparing the parent's own identity for the empty nest.

**Red lines:** authenticity rule on applications is absolute; the AI never chooses the teen's path — it illuminates tradeoffs and reflects the teen's own signals back; mental-health protocols remain fully active (this is peak-risk age); no data about the teen is shared with parents beyond the published rules, even when parents demand it — we lose the account before we betray the teen (see `06 §4` for the safety exceptions).

---

## 9. S7 · Launch (18+): from system to network

**Developmental jobs:** independent living, career entry or higher education, financial footing, adult relationships, continued reinvention.

**AI role & behaviors:** the relationship graduates — the Companion becomes a peer strategist the young adult owns outright (their data, their terms). Career navigation, skill reinvention cycles, first-job coaching, and the alumni network: the cohort of children who grew up in the system becomes each other's professional and social network — the ultimate Depth-tier asset. Parents' subscription sunsets into an optional light-touch family layer.

**The graduation test (the system's final exam, per principle 2):** the young adult can run their life *without us* — we measure whether the exported habits, judgment, relationships, and portfolio stand on their own.

## 10. Cross-stage threads (the through-lines every agent maintains)

1. **The interest spiral:** interests are detected early, deepened continuously, and allowed to die honorably. The Child Model tracks the full history so no enthusiasm is ever wasted — the S3 bug obsession becomes S5 biology becomes S6 research internship, or it doesn't, and that's fine too.
2. **The finishing muscle:** from S3's first finished comic to S6's shipped venture, *completion* is trained as a distinct, celebrated skill at every stage.
3. **The verification instinct:** "spot the AI's mistake" (S3) → labeled AI collaboration (S4) → source criticism (S5) → professional-grade AI direction (S6). One competency, four altitudes.
4. **The privacy escalator:** parent sees everything (S1–S3) → parent sees summaries (S4) → parent sees themes + safety flags (S5) → parent sees what the teen chooses + safety floor (S6) → nothing without consent (S7). Published, predictable, never renegotiated ad hoc.
5. **The human-connection ratchet:** every stage adds a *human* relationship layer the AI actively built: parent serve-and-return → playmates → friends → mentors → networks. Audited quarterly per principle 3.
6. **The struggle contract:** at every stage, some of the work is hard on purpose, the child knows why, the parent knows why, and the satisfaction instrument (`05`) is designed so that honest struggle doesn't read as product failure.
