# 05 · Measurement System

What we measure, how the 90% satisfaction commitment is instrumented, how tier progression is judged, and the anti-gaming rules that keep all of it honest.

---

## 1. The metric hierarchy

Four layers, in rank order. When layers conflict, the higher layer wins the argument:

1. **Child outcomes** (lagging, the point): flourishing pillars (`01 §5`), domain mastery growth, tier progression, and post-graduation life signals (S7 cohort studies).
2. **Family satisfaction** (the 90% commitment): child + parent instruments below.
3. **Leading indicators** (what predicts 1 and 2): visible-progress events, human-connection ledger, struggle calibration hit-rate, loop adherence.
4. **Engagement** (diagnostic only): session completion, digest opens, retention. Engagement is read as *evidence about* the layers above, never optimized for itself — a rise in child session time with a flat human-connection ledger is a red flag, not a win.

---

## 2. The satisfaction instrument (the 90% commitment, precisely)

**Definition:** ≥90% of active families rate satisfied-or-better on the monthly pulse, measured separately for children and parents, reported separately per stage, with non-responders followed up (a silent family is not assumed satisfied).

**Parent pulse (monthly, 3 questions, <60 seconds):**
1. "How is this working for your family?" (5-point: great / good / okay / not well / poorly) — *the* satisfaction number: satisfied = top 2 boxes.
2. "Can you see your child growing in a way you care about?" (yes clearly / somewhat / not yet) — the visible-progress check.
3. One open text: "What should we do differently?" — feeds the root-cause taxonomy (`04 §8`).

**Child pulse (monthly, in-session, age-adapted):**
- S3–S4: three-face scale on "How do you feel about our time together?" plus one playful open question ("if you were the boss of me, what would you change?"). Satisfied = top 2 faces.
- S5–S6: two questions, teen-toned: "Worth your time this month?" (definitely / mostly / meh / no) and "What's one thing I should stop or start?" Satisfied = top 2.
- S1–S2: the child metric is a parent-reported proxy: "Does your child seem happier/more engaged in daily life this month?"

**Reporting rules:**
- The public number is the *minimum* across (child, parent) × (stages with ≥30 active families) — the system is only as good as its weakest constituency.
- Struggle-week responses are *included*, not excluded — the design must absorb honest struggle (`04 §8.6`), not launder it out of the data.
- Churned families get an exit measurement; voluntary churn for unresolved dissatisfaction counts against the month.

**The math of 90%:** at 90%+, roughly one family in ten is unhappy at any time — so the detractor SLA (`04 §8.2`) is not an edge-case process; it is a core, permanently staffed function.

## 3. Flourishing & development metrics (layer 1)

Tracked per child in the Child Model; reviewed quarterly; reported to families as narrative, to the team as data.

| Pillar | Instrument | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Positive emotion | Child pulse trend + parent report | Monthly |
| Engagement/flow | In-session flow indicators (persistence past difficulty, voluntary return to task) | Continuous |
| Relationships | **Human-connection ledger**: count of system-created human interactions (conversations sparked, playdates, showcases, mentor contacts) vs. child screen-hours consumed; friendship & mentor presence by stage | Quarterly audit |
| Meaning | Projects shipped to real audiences; contribution events | Monthly |
| Accomplishment | Domain mastery deltas (criterion-referenced); portfolio growth; "couldn't → can" narratives | Quarterly |
| Autonomy | Self-initiated projects/sessions ratio; age-appropriate decision ownership | Quarterly |
| Vitality | Sleep/movement/outdoor parent-or-teen reports (light-touch) | Monthly |

**Wellbeing floor:** validated screeners (age-appropriate, e.g. brief mood/anxiety instruments at S5+) offered on schedule and on signal; results are clinical-pathway data (`06 §6–7`), never product KPIs.

## 4. Tier progression (Foundation → Craft → Frontier)

Criterion-referenced per `01 §3`; the Planner's tier engine tracks distance-to-criterion, humans certify transitions.

**Foundation ("Flourishing") certification — all six domains green for age:**
Body: healthy sleep/movement habits · Heart: age-typical regulation + demonstrated resilience event · People: ≥1 close friendship (S3+), family connection intact · Mind: core mastery at/above grade band with durable retention · Hands: ≥4 finished projects/year, ≥1 self-initiated · Compass: articulable values + honest-conduct track record. **Target: every child in the system reaches and holds Foundation** — this is the "top 5%" promise, and it is achievable by design because it is criterion-based.

**Craft ("1%") certification — Foundation held ≥1 year, plus in one chosen domain:**
deliberate-practice depth (documented hours + coach-graded quality curve) · external validation (audience, competition, publication, users, revenue — any one) · ≥1 real human mentor in the domain · demonstrated AI-leverage in the craft (can brief, direct, and verify AI work in their field).

**Frontier ("0.1%") certification — Craft held, plus:**
an original contribution others value (shipped venture, research, body of work, institution) · self-authored direction (the teen, not the parent or the system, is steering) · adult-grade network (practitioners who treat them as a colleague). Frontier is rare *by nature of the criterion*, not by quota — we never cap it, and we never promise it: we promise the path and the coaching.

**Guardrails:** tier language is parent/team-facing by default; children see quests and next-criterion distance. A tier can be paused (never "lost") when life happens. Craft/Frontier pushes that cost Foundation pillars (sleep, friendship, joy) are vetoed by the Planner's balance check — *the tiers stack, they don't trade*.

## 5. Leading indicators (layer 3 — the weekly dashboard)

- **Visible-progress events/family/month** (artifact shipped, milestone narrated, before/after shown) — the single strongest satisfaction predictor; target ≥2.
- **Struggle calibration hit-rate:** % of session challenges landing in the 70–85% success band.
- **Human-connection ratio** (ledger events per child screen-hour) — must stay above stage-specific floors.
- **Memory quality:** % of sessions containing a correct, natural callback to the child's life/history (audited by sampling) — the "feeling known" driver.
- **Loop adherence:** digest read-rate, pulse response-rate, project ship-rate, recovery-SLA hit-rate.
- **Expectation integrity:** % of new families who can correctly describe the struggle contract at day 30 (sampled) — the upstream fix for most future detractors.

## 6. Anti-gaming rules (Goodhart defenses)

1. **Paired metrics:** every target metric ships with a counter-metric watched at equal rank (satisfaction ↔ struggle calibration; engagement ↔ human-connection ratio; mastery velocity ↔ retention-after-delay; tier progression ↔ wellbeing floor). Moving the target while the pair degrades counts as a miss.
2. **No agent sees the scoreboard:** agents are never prompted with satisfaction targets or retention goals; they run the developmental playbook, and we measure whether the playbook works. (An AI optimizing a child's survey answers is the exact failure mode `01 §4.6` bans.)
3. **Narrative audits:** quarterly, humans read full longitudinal samples (privacy-scoped) and answer one question: "Is this child actually better off?" Sampled truth outranks aggregated proxies.
4. **Gamed-metric protocol:** any metric found gamed is frozen, redesigned, and its historical wins re-based — celebrated gaming compounds; confessed gaming teaches.
5. **Cohort honesty:** outcome claims are reported against matched external baselines where possible; we do not claim credit for selection effects (families who choose a system like this differ from average) — internally we know the difference even where marketing may blur it.
6. **Experiments:** pedagogy changes ship as flagged experiments with pre-registered success criteria including a wellbeing no-harm bar; children are never in experiments that withhold known-strong practices (no child gets the no-phonics arm).
