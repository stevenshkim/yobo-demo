# 04 · Operating Playbook

The runbook: how the system behaves day to day, week to week, and at every critical moment in a family's journey. Where `02` says *what* children need and `03` says *who* delivers it, this document says *when and how*.

---

## 1. Onboarding: the first 30 days decide the relationship

**Day 0 — Intake as conversation, not forms.** The Parent Co-pilot interviews the parent (and, S4+, the child separately): family rhythms, values, worries, the child's story so far, what "growing up well" means to *this* family. Output: Child Model v0 + a written **Family Charter** — goals, boundaries, tech values, and the struggle contract (`02 §10.6`) in plain language the family co-signs.

**Day 1–7 — One win fast.** Each user gets a felt win in week one:
- Parent (any stage): one piece of advice specific enough to their child that it could not have come from a book or a generic chatbot.
- Child (S3+): a first session that ends with "I did something hard and it felt good," plus the first entry in their portfolio.

**Day 8–30 — Establish the loops.** Daily/weekly rhythms (below) become habitual; the first monthly project ships (S3+); the first digest lands; the first satisfaction pulse is taken and *responded to visibly* — families learn early that their feedback moves the product.

**Expectation-setting is onboarding's real job.** Families are told explicitly, in the charter: progress will be visible monthly, not daily; some sessions will be hard on purpose and here's why; the AI will sometimes decline things (do homework for the child, extend screen time) and here's why; here is exactly what data we keep and who sees what. Families who understand the model rate it dramatically higher — most dissatisfaction is a broken expectation, so we set them precisely.

## 2. The daily loop

| Stage | Child-facing | Parent-facing |
|---|---|---|
| S1–S2 | — | One micro-nudge max (a play invitation or rhythm tip), skippable, never guilt-inducing |
| S3 | 10–20 min session (reading-anchored), joyful hard stop, off-screen hand-off | Optional one-line "today's spark" |
| S4 | 20–40 min: practice block + project block | Same |
| S5–S6 | Self-scheduled; the AI protects sleep and pushes back on 11pm sessions | Nothing daily (respect the teen's space) |

**Session design rules (every stage):**
1. Open with connection (30 seconds of genuine check-in, memory-informed: "did the volcano thing work?").
2. One clear challenge at the learning edge; the child works, the AI scaffolds (contract rule 4).
3. Close with reflection + a named micro-win + hand-off to the physical world.
4. End on time, end on a high. The last 60 seconds are engineered to be the best 60 seconds — memory of the session is disproportionately made there, and that memory *is* the child-satisfaction score.

---

## 3. The weekly & monthly loops

**Weekly (the system's heartbeat):**
- Observer compiles the week → Planner adjusts next week → **Parent digest** ships: exactly three items — one insight ("she started self-correcting mid-sentence — that's a big fluency milestone"), one conversation-starter for the dinner table, one heads-up or ask. Two-minute read, zero dashboard homework.
- Community Connector proposes the week's human move (playdate, showcase invite, mentor ping).
- Child check-in (S3+): 30-second mood/energy pulse inside a session, age-appropriate instrument (`05 §2`).

**Monthly:**
- Project ships (S3–S6) → real audience → celebration ritual → portfolio entry.
- **Progress narrative** to the family: plain-language story of the month with evidence (artifacts, before/after samples) — *narrative beats dashboards for satisfaction and for truth*.
- Satisfaction pulse (both users) + recovery protocol on any low score (§8).

**Quarterly:**
- Growth-plan review: Planner's next-quarter arc, previewed with parent (S1–S4) or negotiated with teen first (S5+).
- Human-connection audit: did we create more human interaction than we consumed? (principle 3; metric in `05 §3`).
- Six-domain balance check with rebalancing moves.

---

## 4. Stage transitions: the rite-of-passage moments

Transitions are the highest-risk, highest-trust moments; each gets a designed experience, not a config flip the family discovers by accident:

- **S2→S3 (first child sessions):** parent orientation first (what sessions are, the struggle contract, how to talk about them); child's first session co-piloted with the parent present; Companion introduces itself honestly ("I'm a computer helper — I know a lot and I also make mistakes, and you get to catch them").
- **S4→S5 (the re-contract):** separate conversations with teen and parent; new privacy terms read *to both* in the same words; teen signs their own charter for the first time. The parent conversation is the harder one — the Co-pilot runs the "manager → consultant" transition module for weeks around it.
- **S6→S7 (graduation):** the Child Model is formally handed to the young adult (export + ownership transfer ceremony); the graduation test (`02 §9`) is run and shared; alumni network invitation.

---

## 5. The Family Tech Charter (the product's Trojan gift)

At S2 and again at S4, the Co-pilot guides the family through writing their technology charter — screens, phones, social media, gaming, AI use — *before* each pressure wave hits. We provide the evidence base and facilitation; the family makes the calls; the charter becomes enforceable family law that the child helped write (which is what makes it hold). Our own product's limits are written into it, on the same page as everyone else's — the AI that limits itself is the AI parents trust.

---

## 6. Disagreeing with parents (the gracious-pushback protocol)

When a parent asks for something developmentally harmful, the Co-pilot runs V-E-A-R:
1. **Validate** the underlying goal ("you want her reading early because reading is everything — you're right that it is").
2. **Evidence**, briefly and without condescension ("at 4, the strongest predictor of age-8 reading is oral language and being read to; worksheet drills at this age show no lasting advantage and can dent motivation").
3. **Alternative** that serves the same goal ("here's a 10-minute daily read-aloud + word-play routine that *is* early reading instruction, disguised as fun").
4. **Respect** the final call within safety bounds — record it, revisit gently with the child's own data later ("remember the sound-games route we chose? here's what happened to her letter knowledge since").

Hard limits that survive any parent demand: safety protocols, privacy promises to the child, the no-doing-the-child's-work rule, and stage session caps. These are contractual, stated at onboarding, and enforced kindly but absolutely.

## 7. Content & pedagogy standards

- **Mastery-based progression everywhere:** advancement on demonstrated mastery; review scheduled by spaced-retrieval; struggle calibrated to ZPD (target success rate roughly 70–85% on new material — hard enough to grow, kind enough to sustain).
- **Interest-woven academics:** the curriculum library is tagged so any skill can be practiced through any interest; the Planner threads them.
- **Evidence tiers:** every pedagogical policy in the library is tagged strong/moderate/emerging evidence; strong-evidence practices (phonics-based early reading, retrieval practice, worked examples → fading) are defaults; emerging practices ship only as flagged experiments (`05 §6`).
- **Voice:** one Companion voice per stage version; short sentences; genuine, specific warmth; zero corporate cheer; humor that respects the child's intelligence.
- **Localization is developmental, not just linguistic:** family structures, schooling systems, and cultural values differ; the charter process (§1) is where the family's own values are loaded into the system rather than ours imposed.

---

## 8. The 90% engine: satisfaction operations

Satisfaction is operated, not hoped for. The full instrument is in `05 §2`; the operating protocol:

1. **Continuous sensing:** monthly pulses (both users), in-session sentiment (children), digest engagement, unsolicited-signal mining (support tickets, cancellations, session-skipping streaks).
2. **Detractor SLA:** any low pulse or churn-risk signal → human-touch response within 48h (Human Desk or founder while small). The response is diagnostic, not defensive: what did we miss, what expectation broke, what would earn the next month?
3. **Root-cause taxonomy:** every detractor case is coded — broken expectation / progress not visible / struggle miscalibrated / relationship miss (tone, memory failure) / logistics friction / price-value. Each code has a named fix-owner and a monthly trend review.
4. **The two honest levers:** (a) make progress *more visible* (better narratives, more before/after evidence) and (b) make care *more felt* (memory-informed touches, faster recovery). We never pull the dishonest levers (inflate praise, remove struggle, tell families what they want to hear) — see `05 §6` for the anti-gaming rules.
5. **Close the loop publicly:** monthly "you said, we changed" note to all families. Feedback that visibly moves the product is itself a top-3 satisfaction driver.
6. **Struggle-week protection:** when the Planner schedules a deliberately hard stretch, the Co-pilot pre-briefs the parent ("this week will feel harder — here's what we're building"), converting the predictable satisfaction dip into demonstrated expertise.

## 9. Failure playbooks (the misses that will happen)

- **The child is bored:** treat as data, not resistance — challenge level, interest match, or session shape is wrong; L0/L1 escalation; never respond with reward-mechanics sugar.
- **The child tries to make the AI do their homework:** the refusal is warm and skill-building ("I can't write it for you — but give me your worst first sentence and I'll show you how editors make bad sentences great"). Parent is *not* narced on for a first offense; patterns surface in the digest as a coaching topic.
- **The child says something concerning:** Sentinel protocols (`06 §6`), always. The Companion never handles crisis alone.
- **The parent disengages** (digests unread, no charter follow-through): Co-pilot drops to minimum-viable-parent mode — shorter nudges, more child-side scaffolding, no guilt — and keeps the door open; a disengaged parent is a context, not a lost cause.
- **We were wrong about the child** (misjudged level, mislabeled interest): say so, to the child, in the session ("I thought that would be easy for you — I was wrong, and that's on me"). The system modeling how to be wrong gracefully *is* the character curriculum.
- **Extended absence** (illness, family crisis, vacation): re-entry protocol — no guilt, no streak-shaming, a warm "here's where we left off" and a deliberately easy first session back.

---

## 10. Operating principles for the team

1. Ship narratives, not dashboards, to families; keep the dashboards for us.
2. Every new feature must name the loop it lives in (§2–3) or it will die unused.
3. When in doubt between more AI and more human, choose the option that produces more human (principle 3).
4. The Companion's memory of small things ("how was the recital?") outperforms any feature we can build — invest in memory quality relentlessly.
5. Never ship to a stage cohort ahead of its `05` quality bars; a burned family at S3 never gives us S5.
